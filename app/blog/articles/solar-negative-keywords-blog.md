# Stop Burning Money: 50 Essential Negative Keywords Every Solar Installer Needs

*Your Google Ads budget is leaking thousands every month on clicks that will never convert—and you probably don't even realize it.*

If you've been running Google Ads for your solar installation business, you're likely familiar with that sinking feeling when you check your campaign metrics. High spend, decent click volume, but conversion rates that make you question if your targeting is broken. 

Well, I've got news for you: it probably is—but not in the way you think. The problem isn't just who you're targeting, but who you're *not* excluding.

Think of negative keywords like a bouncer for your ad campaigns. Without them, anyone can stroll in and drain your budget—from high school students working on science projects to DIY enthusiasts who have no intention of hiring a professional installer. 

Let's fix that today.

## Why Negative Keywords Are Your Secret Weapon

Google's keyword matching has gotten increasingly broad over the years. When you bid on terms like "solar installation" or "solar panels," Google might show your ads for searches like "how to install solar panels myself" or "solar panel science fair project."

See the problem? These clicks cost the same as qualified leads, but they'll never convert.

Here's what makes this particularly painful for solar installers:

- The average cost-per-click for solar terms hovers around $6-12 (and up to $20+ in competitive markets)
- Solar has a broad appeal, attracting researchers, students, and DIY enthusiasts—not just qualified homeowners
- Many solar-related searches have dual intent (educational vs. commercial)

One installer we worked with was burning through $1,400 monthly—nearly 40% of their ad budget—on clicks from people who were never going to convert. After implementing a proper negative keyword strategy, their cost per qualified lead dropped from $95 to $58.

But negative keywords don't just save money. They actually *improve* campaign performance by:

- Boosting your Quality Score (Google rewards ads that are relevant to searchers)
- Increasing your click-through rate (by appearing on more relevant searches)
- Improving conversion rates (by filtering out non-prospects before they click)

Now let's get to the good stuff: the actual keywords you need to block.

## Pro-Tip: How to Identify Your Own Campaign-Specific Negative Keywords

The list above gives you a solid foundation, but your business will have unique terms to exclude based on your specific services and local market conditions.

Here's how to find them:

1. Open your Google Ads account and navigate to "Search Terms" report
2. Sort by cost (highest to lowest) 
3. Look for terms with:
   - High impressions but low CTR
   - Decent click volume but no conversions
   - Clearly non-commercial intent

Pay special attention to any terms that have racked up significant spend without conversions. These are your low-hanging fruit.

I recommend doing this analysis bi-weekly for new campaigns and monthly for established ones. Set a calendar reminder—this simple 15-minute task can save you thousands.

## Beyond the Basics: Advanced Negative Keyword Strategies

Once you've implemented the basics, consider these advanced strategies:

### Negative Keyword Lists vs. Campaign-Level Exclusions

Create separate negative keyword lists for:
- Brand campaigns
- Generic service campaigns
- Local/geo campaigns

This gives you more control and prevents cross-contamination between campaigns with different goals.

### Seasonal Considerations

Certain negative keywords become more important during specific times of year:

- School project terms spike in January and September
- DIY searches increase in spring and summer
- "Solar tax credit" searches spike near year-end

Adjust your negatives accordingly, or set up automated rules to activate certain lists during these periods.

### Geographic Considerations

If you serve multiple markets, consider region-specific negative keywords:

- Exclude names of utilities you don't service
- Exclude neighborhoods outside your service area
- Exclude states/cities where you don't have licensing
